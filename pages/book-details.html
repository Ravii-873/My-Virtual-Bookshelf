1984

George Orwell

★ ★ ★ ★ ★

19/06/2019

What struck me most about 1984 wasn't the dystopia itself — surveillance states, propaganda, and totalitarian control are familiar territory in fiction by now. What stayed with me was how quietly plausible Orwell makes the mechanics of it feel. The Party doesn't just punish dissent; it makes dissent structurally unthinkable. Newspeak isn't a gimmick, it's the novel's sharpest idea: if you strip the words for freedom out of a language, you don't need to ban the concept, because no one can formulate the thought anymore. That's a genuinely unsettling premise, and Orwell commits to it with a kind of clinical patience rather than melodrama.

Winston Smith is not a hero in any conventional sense, and I think that's deliberate and effective. He's tired, compromised, and his rebellion is small and almost pathetic — a diary, an affair, a half-formed hope that the proles might eventually rise. Reading it, I kept expecting the plot to reward that hope, because that's what most stories do. Orwell refuses. Room 101 isn't just torture, it's the systematic dismantling of the last private thing Winston has: his own mind. The final image, of Winston loving Big Brother, is one of the bleakest endings I've encountered, precisely because there's no ambiguity left to cling to. The system wins completely.

If I have a criticism, it's that the middle section — Goldstein's book-within-a-book — slows the pacing considerably. It's intellectually interesting (the theory of perpetual war as an economic stabilizer is genuinely sharp political thinking) but it reads more like an essay than fiction, and it stalls the emotional momentum Orwell had built.

Still, what makes 1984 endure isn't the specific technology or the Cold War politics — it's the psychological argument underneath: that control over language and memory is control over reality itself. That idea hasn't aged at all, which is exactly why the book still gets reached for, correctly or not, whenever surveillance or misinformation comes up today.
